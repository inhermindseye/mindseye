Women at the Olympics, 1896-2026

women
sport
Changing female participation at the Olympic Games over time, by sport
Author

Sharon Howard

Published

15 November 2026

I’ve been playing with the OlympicAthletes data package. I liked this little Observable Notebook visualising women’s participation in the Olympics, but wanted to expand on it to explore development among different sports. These heatmaps are the result.

Sports that were more recent additions to the Games tend to be more gender-balanced from the start, while there’s considerable variation among more long-established sports. Figure skating, for example, was close to parity almost from its inception, and swimming and diving weren’t too far behind, whereas cycling had no female participation at all until the 1980s (which surprised me a bit). Even within related categories there can be differences: women have constituted the majority of competitors in dressage for half a century, but show jumping and eventing remain predominantly male.

Notes

I’ve removed a fairly small number of sports that featured in only a single Games, all in the early years. (Three sports are split across Summer and Winter Games.)

The Games weren’t held in 1916, 1940 or 1944 and those years are omitted from the heatmap. White squares indicate that a sport was not present.

I’ve noticed a few errors in the data, but it seems to be largely accurate. It’s an exceptionally rich dataset that I plan to do more with in the future.

sports_sex =
ojsSportsSex
        .map(d => ({...d, p_women: d.women/d.total, p_men: d.men/d.total, date: new Date(d.year, 0, 1) }) )



sports_start_end =  
d3.flatRollup(sports_sex, v => d3.extent(v, d => d.date), d => d.sport)
    .map((n) => ({sport: n[0], start: n[1][0], ends:n[1][1]}))
    
// d3.sort to sort by multiple variables... 

sort_sports =
    d3.sort(sports_start_end, ({start}) => start, ({ends}) => ends, ({sport}) => sport)
    .map(d => d.sport)
    


sports_summer = sports_sex.filter(d=>d.season=="Summer")

sports_winter = sports_sex.filter(d=>d.season=="Winter")

// summer only (for heatmap), using year instead of date. can add date afterwards if needed.

summer_start_end =

    .map((n) => ({sport: n[0], start: n[1][0], ends:n[1][1]}))
    //.map(d => ({...d, date: new Date(d.start, 0, 1) }) )
